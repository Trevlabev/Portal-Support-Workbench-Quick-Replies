Portal Support Training — Trainer Guide and Answer Key

This guide supports coaching and self-review. It does not establish an official pass/fail standard or replace current manager direction.

Suggested delivery

  • Use Phase 1 before independent ticket handling.
  • Demonstrate systems with a safe training account or approved example.
  • Ask the learner to distinguish Portal-side, docket-side, payment-side, and unverified facts.
  • Grade reasoning and documentation, not memorization alone.
  • Reconfirm current approval criteria, Cause Codes, county guidance, technical limits, and financial paths.

Knowledge-check answer key

Q01 — Role boundaries and source hierarchy

Answer: B. The manager-provided workflow remains primary unless the team explicitly changes it
The training source hierarchy treats manager-provided operational instructions as primary. Public material is supplemental unless the team reconciles or changes the procedure.

Q02 — Role boundaries and source hierarchy

Answer: B. Provide technical/navigation assistance and refer form selection to the applicable Clerk
Form selection and court procedure are outside Portal Support’s technical/navigation scope.

Q03 — Role boundaries and source hierarchy

Answer: B. Only after current confirmation and only for that county
County behavior may change independently and must not be generalized.

Q04 — Ivanti ticket handling and documentation

Answer: B. Recording what the system actually showed
The Workbench requires a finding; a checked system without a finding does not establish evidence.

Q05 — Ivanti ticket handling and documentation

Answer: B. Internal system findings and escalation detail
Internal findings and escalation detail belong in the internal note. Customer communication should contain only actionable and appropriate information.

Q06 — Ivanti ticket handling and documentation

Answer: B. The current manager-approved code for the actual outcome
The exact code can change and must follow current team procedure.

Q07 — Customer communication, Quick Messages, and Response Builder

Answer: B. Add relevant messages, remove duplicate sections, and edit the final draft to the ticket
Quick Messages are editable building blocks, not automatic final responses.

Q08 — Customer communication, Quick Messages, and Response Builder

Answer: B. Review before use
The label indicates wording with flags, uncertainty, changeable procedure, or other audit concerns.

Q09 — Customer communication, Quick Messages, and Response Builder

Answer: B. The draft should remain intact and be marked stale until the analyst chooses to regenerate
The Workbench protects manual edits and requires an intentional regeneration.

Q10 — Registration workflow

Answer: B. Save the ticket
The manager-provided registration instructions explicitly call for saving immediately after the filer-role selection.

Q11 — Registration workflow

Answer: B. RAC → Menu → Security → Registration Applications
The registration workflow uses RAC Registration Applications.

Q12 — Registration workflow

Answer: B. Use current manager-approved criteria
The source supports the workflow but not a complete current criteria list; current manager guidance is required.

Q13 — Florida Bar verification and attorney-account checks

The exact profile pattern ends with profile/?num=[numeric Bar number].

Q14 — Florida Bar verification and attorney-account checks

Answer: C. Only that the two displayed email values differ and the manager procedure must be followed
A mismatch is a factual comparison, not an automatic authorization or account decision.

Q15 — Florida Bar verification and attorney-account checks

Answer: A. The parser may misread page text and the official profile must be visually reviewed
Local parsing is a convenience. Human review against the official page remains necessary.

Q16 — Account access, password, inactive, and locked accounts

Answer: C. The username and associated email, but not the password
The workflow records account identifiers and status but prohibits requesting or recording passwords.

Q17 — Account access, password, inactive, and locked accounts

Answer: A. It may actually reflect incorrect credentials and requires RAC verification
The manager source notes that an inactive message may appear when credentials are entered incorrectly; the actual RAC status must be checked.

Q18 — Account access, password, inactive, and locked accounts

Answer: B. When Tier I cannot safely correct it after the current procedure and the handoff is complete
Escalation follows verification and the manager-approved Tier I procedure unless current direction requires otherwise.

Q19 — Filing status, Workbench, My Submissions, and docket separation

Answer: C. Only that unfinished/history-like activity exists; it does not prove submission
Manager-provided wording explicitly separates Workbench activity from submitted filings.

Q20 — Filing status, Workbench, My Submissions, and docket separation

Answer: C. The appropriate official docket evidence, such as CCIS/Clerk-controlled record
Portal status and official docket status must be verified separately.

Q21 — Filing status, Workbench, My Submissions, and docket separation

Answer: B. The Portal status and docket status are separate; the Clerk controls official processing
The role boundary requires a factual explanation of the two systems and Clerk ownership.

Q22 — My Cases, document access, eService, and service lists

Answer: B. It may still contain the prior address and must be checked separately
The source notes that profile changes do not automatically update existing service lists or Clerk records.

Q23 — My Cases, document access, eService, and service lists

Answer: B. Whether the problem is case population, docket access, eService, service-list maintenance, or subscription
Those functions have different systems, evidence, and ownership.

Q24 — My Cases, document access, eService, and service lists

Answer: B. The applicable Clerk/court record process
Portal Support verifies and explains, but official docket access is Clerk-controlled.

Q25 — Upload, PDF, browser, and clean-slate troubleshooting

Answer: B. Start the filing over and do not reuse the failed Workbench item
The manager troubleshooting sequence requires a fresh filing and no reuse of the failed Workbench item.

Q26 — Upload, PDF, browser, and clean-slate troubleshooting

Answer: B. Exact error, date/time, environment, document details, scope, impact, screenshots, and completed results
A complete handoff must prevent the Analyst from repeating the Tier I investigation.

Q27 — Upload, PDF, browser, and clean-slate troubleshooting

Answer: A. Whether the upload indicator fully completed before adding another document, plus the clean retry and document conditions
The upload workflow specifically includes waiting for full completion and applying the clean-slate/document-validation sequence.

Q28 — Payments, unknown charges, receipts, and refunds

Answer: B. Last four digits, payment type, exact amount/date, account holder, and filing/descriptor information
The payment workflow limits collection to the information needed for safe transaction research.

Q29 — Payments, unknown charges, receipts, and refunds

Answer: A. TPE and the related filing in RAC/My Submissions
The transaction and the associated filing must both be researched.

Q30 — Payments, unknown charges, receipts, and refunds

Answer: B. Only after ownership and eligibility are verified—and the analyst should still use current approved wording rather than promise
The manager workflow explicitly prohibits promising a refund before ownership and eligibility are verified.

Q31 — Correction Queue, wrong attorney/county/case, and accepted filings

Answer: B. The applicable Clerk
Accepted or docketed changes are Clerk-controlled.

Q32 — Correction Queue, wrong attorney/county/case, and accepted filings

Answer: B. RAC shows Portal status while CCIS supports official docket/case findings
The two systems answer different ownership and status questions.

Q33 — Correction Queue, wrong attorney/county/case, and accepted filings

Answer: B. Complete the applicable payment troubleshooting and document the results
The correction workflow requires payment troubleshooting before an unresolved payment failure is escalated.

Q34 — Known Issues, Master Tickets, and Analyst escalation

Answer: B. A one-sentence statement of the exact problem and affected function, followed by identifiers and evidence
The technical workflow requires a concise problem statement and a structured evidence package.

Q35 — Known Issues, Master Tickets, and Analyst escalation

Answer: B. After symptoms, function, and evidence support the match under current procedure
Known Issues are possible matches, not automatic associations.

Q36 — Known Issues, Master Tickets, and Analyst escalation

Answer: A. A requested Analyst action
The receiving Analyst needs to know what review or action is being requested.

Scenario coaching answers

Scenario 1: Pending attorney registration with missing phone

Decision 1: B. Assign the incident, select the filer role, save, and update the required ticket/customer fields
Registration begins with incident ownership, filer-role selection/save, and ticket/customer preparation.

Decision 2: B. The pending registration in RAC
The registration source states that missing address/phone may be retrieved from the pending RAC registration.

Decision 3: B. Current manager-approved approval criteria
The source does not provide a complete criteria list; current manager guidance controls.

Coaching points

  • Complete the Ivanti customer record before closeout.
  • Document the RAC decision and communication.

Model work-note structure

Incident assigned. Filer role selected and ticket fields updated. Existing customer record reviewed; missing telephone obtained from the pending RAC application and customer record updated. Registration application reviewed under current manager-approved criteria. Decision, customer communication, resolution note, and Cause Code documented.

Scenario 2: Registration rejected for a correctable issue

Decision 1: B. Red X with the reason, then save
The registration workflow uses the red X, recorded reason, and save for a rejection.

Decision 2: B. The rejection reason and required correction
The customer must receive the actual reason and what must be corrected.

Decision 3: A. Changing status to Resolved, adding resolution notes, selecting the current Cause Code, and saving
Those are the source-supported closeout steps.

Coaching points

  • Do not invent or soften the RAC reason into a different requirement.
  • Do not resolve before sending the rejection communication.

Model work-note structure

RAC registration reviewed under current manager-approved criteria. Application rejected using the red X; reason entered and saved. Customer advised of the rejection reason and required correction. Incident resolved with resolution notes and current Cause Code.

Scenario 3: Florida Bar email does not match the ticket

Decision 1: B. A factual difference requiring the current manager account procedure
The mismatch does not independently establish authorization or the correct account action.

Decision 2: B. Displayed name/status/eligibility, public email comparison, and the action taken
Document only the verification fields needed for the ticket.

Decision 3: B. No, it must be checked against the official page
The local parser is not a substitute for human review.

Coaching points

  • Use the official exact profile.
  • Do not over-interpret the email mismatch.

Model work-note structure

Official Florida Bar profile reviewed for the provided Bar number. Displayed member details and public email were compared with the ticket/account information. Email values differ. Current manager-approved attorney-account verification procedure followed; no credential information recorded.

Scenario 4: Locked-account message after repeated attempts

Decision 1: B. Verify requester authorization and the RAC account/status
The actual account and requester must be verified first.

Decision 2: A. Conflicting old password requirements
The content audit excluded conflicting password requirements from active procedure.

Decision 3: B. After the current locked-account procedure when Tier I cannot safely correct it
The complete Tier I record should precede escalation unless current management directs otherwise.

Coaching points

  • Do not request or record a password.
  • Document RAC status and every action.

Model work-note structure

Requester authorization verified. RAC account located and username, associated email, filer role, and actual status recorded. Current manager-approved locked-account review completed. No password or credential information collected. [Action/outcome or Analyst escalation reason].

Scenario 5: Item appears only in Workbench

Decision 1: B. Only unfinished/history-like activity, not submission
Workbench is not proof of submission or timestamp.

Decision 2: B. Start the filing again from the beginning and do not use the failed/stale Workbench item
The manager procedure directs a clean restart when no submitted filing exists.

Decision 3: B. Workbench finding, My Submissions finding, absence of filing number, and customer next action
The work note should make the evidence and conclusion reviewable.

Coaching points

  • Use “filing number” precisely.
  • Do not call a Workbench identifier a submitted filing.

Model work-note structure

Customer reported a Workbench item. No filing number was available and the item was not found in My Submissions as a submitted filing. Explained that Workbench activity does not prove submission. Customer instructed to begin a new filing rather than resume the Workbench item, consistent with the manager clean-retry guidance.

Scenario 6: Transmitted filing not visible on the docket

Decision 1: A. RAC for Portal status and CCIS/Clerk record for docket status
Portal and docket status must be verified separately.

Decision 2: B. The Clerk
The Portal transmits; the Clerk controls the official docket.

Decision 3: B. Saying Transmitted proves docket placement
RAC status alone does not prove the official docket result.

Coaching points

  • Use exact statuses.
  • Do not merge Portal and docket findings into one conclusion.

Model work-note structure

Filing #________ reviewed in RAC; Portal status displayed as Transmitted. Official docket reviewed separately in CCIS/applicable Clerk record; document not visible at time of review. Explained that Portal transmission and Clerk docket processing are separate. [Referral/escalation based on verified findings].

Scenario 7: Attorney is not receiving eService

Decision 1: A. Profile email and the email on the case service list, plus bounce-back/spam evidence
Existing service lists do not automatically change with the profile.

Decision 2: A. The service list already contains the new profile email
The source specifically warns that profile changes do not automatically update existing service lists.

Decision 3: B. The applicable Clerk
Official record and docket access remain Clerk-controlled.

Coaching points

  • Classify delivery versus access.
  • Document both email values and any bounce-back evidence.

Model work-note structure

Issue classified as case-specific eService delivery. Portal profile email and available case service-list information compared; profile update does not establish that the existing service list changed. Spam/junk and bounce-back conditions reviewed. [Service-list procedure or Clerk referral] provided and findings documented.

Scenario 8: Upload reaches 100% and stalls

Decision 1: A. Use a supported browser, sign out, start over, and do not reuse the Workbench item
Multiple manager-supported clean-retry conditions are present.

Decision 2: A. Wait for the upload indicator to fully complete and validate PDF/security/filename/page conditions
The upload workflow combines session and document validation.

Decision 3: A. An evidence-complete Analyst package
Exact error, environment, document details, scope, impact, and results are needed.

Coaching points

  • Do not split the clean-slate steps into contradictory partial messages.
  • Record the result of each retry.

Model work-note structure

Issue reproduced/reported during document upload: first document reached 100% and next document could not be added. Filer was using Safari on Mac and resumed from Workbench. Complete manager-approved clean retry provided: supported browser/computer, Sign Out, hand-keyed site, browser cleanup, new filing from the beginning, no Workbench reuse, document validation, and full upload-completion check. [Results/evidence/escalation].

Scenario 9: Secured PDF fails validation

Decision 1: A. Remove the locked/secured condition and verify the document requirements
The workflow requires an unlocked/unsecured, non-compressed document.

Decision 2: A. 8.5 × 11 page size and other current manager-approved PDF conditions
Page size is part of the manager document-validation sequence.

Decision 3: B. No, verify current manager guidance
The content audit flags changeable technical limits for confirmation.

Coaching points

  • Do not ask the customer for the PDF password.
  • Use current limits, not conflicting historical values.

Model work-note structure

Upload failure reviewed. Document was password protected/secured; page size not confirmed. Customer instructed to create an unlocked/unsecured, non-compressed PDF meeting current manager-approved document requirements, including page size, then complete the clean retry. Result to be documented before escalation.

Scenario 10: Unknown charge with limited information

Decision 1: A. Payment type, last four only, account holder, filing number, and descriptor
The payment workflow collects only the minimum safe identifiers.

Decision 2: A. TPE transaction research and related filing review in RAC/My Submissions
Both the transaction and the filing should be reviewed.

Decision 3: B. “You will definitely receive a refund.”
Refunds must not be promised before ownership and eligibility are verified.

Coaching points

  • Use exact amount/date.
  • Document why ownership was assigned to Portal/banking or the Clerk.

Model work-note structure

Unknown charge reported. Collected payment type, last four only, exact amount/date, account holder, filing number/descriptor when available. Transaction researched in TPE and related filing reviewed in RAC/My Submissions. Ownership and next action documented. No refund promised before verification.

Scenario 11: Accepted filing under the wrong county

Decision 1: B. Refer correction/removal of the accepted/docketed record to the applicable Clerk
Official docket changes are Clerk-controlled.

Decision 2: A. Separate RAC and CCIS findings and the Clerk referral
The two system findings establish the ownership decision.

Decision 3: B. No; provide technical navigation within scope and refer procedural choices to the Clerk
Procedural and legal filing choices remain outside Portal Support scope.

Coaching points

  • Do not send an accepted filing back to Correction Queue.
  • Keep technical navigation separate from court procedure.

Model work-note structure

Filing reviewed in RAC and displayed as Accepted. Official docket reviewed in CCIS and filing was present. Requested change concerns an accepted/docketed filing under the wrong county. Explained that official docket correction/removal is controlled by the applicable Clerk and provided the referral. Portal and docket findings documented separately.

Scenario 12: Multiple users report the same endpoint error

Decision 1: B. Capture evidence for all affected users, search current Known Issues/Master Tickets, and follow the escalation procedure
The multi-user scope is significant, but association still requires evidence and current procedure.

Decision 2: A. Exact error/page/time, identifiers, environments, scope/impact, systems, Tier I results, attachments, and requested Analyst action
The handoff must be evidence-complete and actionable.

Decision 3: B. Use current manager-approved escalation/status wording without promising a repair time
Communicate verified status and avoid unsupported timing or scope claims.

Coaching points

  • Document each affected incident consistently.
  • State what Analyst action is requested.

Model work-note structure

Multiple-user technical issue identified: same endpoint error reported by three organizations within 20 minutes, blocking submission. Exact error, page/action, times, user/filing identifiers, environments, screenshots, scope, impact, system findings, and completed Tier I results documented. Existing Known Issue/Master Ticket search completed under current procedure. Analyst review requested to determine association and corrective action. Customers updated without a repair-time promise.

Scenario 13: Customer asks which summons and filing selections to use

Decision 1: B. Technical/navigation assistance for the website and a referral for forms/procedure/fees
The request crosses the Clerk/legal boundary.

Decision 2: B. Confirm current county guidance and refer the authoritative Clerk process
County requirements may change and are not universal Portal rules.

Decision 3: A. The Portal facts checked, why the issue is Clerk-controlled, and the referral provided
The record should explain the ownership decision.

Coaching points

  • Do not become the source of legal or fee advice.
  • Make the referral specific and useful.

Model work-note structure

Customer requested form, case-type, and county fee guidance. Portal status reviewed only as needed to confirm no unresolved website failure. Explained that Portal Support provides technical/navigation assistance but cannot select forms, determine legal filing choices, or confirm Clerk-controlled fees. Referred customer to the applicable county Clerk and documented the reason.

Scenario 14: Correction Queue payment still fails after saved-method retry

Decision 1: B. No; complete and document the applicable browser/session and payment troubleshooting first unless current management directs otherwise
The workflow requires applicable payment troubleshooting and complete results before escalation.

Decision 2: A. TPE and the filing in RAC/My Submissions
Payment ownership and filing status require both transaction and filing research.

Decision 3: A. The requested Analyst review/action after complete evidence
An actionable handoff states what is needed from the Analyst.

Coaching points

  • Document the exact result of each payment and browser step.
  • Do not include full credentials.

Model work-note structure

Correction Queue payment failure. Saved payment method removed and payment information hand-keyed; failure persisted. TPE transaction research and RAC/My Submissions filing review completed. Full manager-approved browser/session troubleshooting completed and results documented. No full payment credentials collected. Analyst review requested for unresolved Correction Queue payment processing.

Scenario 15: Customer reply assembled from too many templates

Decision 1: B. Remove irrelevant/duplicated blocks and rewrite the final message to the verified issue
Templates require selection, customization, and final review.

Decision 2: A. Acknowledgment, verified finding, applicable PDF/clean-retry steps, and a clear next action
The final response should be concise, relevant, and actionable.

Decision 3: A. Review the final response against the ticket facts and job instructions
The Workbench includes an explicit response-review check for this reason.

Coaching points

  • Manager-provided wording can still be redundant or unsuitable for a specific ticket.
  • Customer and internal content remain separate.

Model work-note structure

Customer response revised to remove duplicated greetings, repeated scope language, and unrelated payment wording. Final response includes the verified PDF upload issue, applicable document and clean-retry steps, customer next action, and appropriate close. Response reviewed against ticket facts and current instructions.
