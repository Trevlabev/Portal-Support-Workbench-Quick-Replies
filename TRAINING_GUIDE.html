Module 1: Role boundaries and source hierarchy

Foundation · 30 minutes

Understand what Portal Support owns, what the Clerk owns, and which source controls when instructions conflict.

Learning objectives

  • Separate Portal technical/navigation support from Clerk-controlled case procedure, docket action, forms, and legal advice.
  • Use manager-provided job instructions as the primary operational source.
  • Recognize county notes, review-pending wording, and changeable technical limits as items that require current confirmation.
  • Collect only the information needed for the support request.

What Portal Support owns

Portal Support verifies Portal-side facts, assists with website navigation and technical use, documents findings, communicates next steps, and routes unresolved technical issues.

  • Portal registration and account access
  • Portal submission and status research
  • My Cases and eService navigation
  • Upload, PDF, browser, and session troubleshooting
  • Payment research and ownership determination
  • Evidence-complete escalation

What Portal Support does not own

The Clerk controls the official docket and county procedure. Portal Support should verify enough information to establish ownership, then give a clear referral.

  • Selecting legal forms or filing choices
  • Giving legal advice or case strategy
  • Changing or removing official docket entries
  • Providing official case status beyond verified system facts
  • Performing Clerk, Judge, or court-office duties

Source priority

Use the team’s operational sources in this order. Do not silently replace manager procedure with public documentation.

  • 1. Manager-provided job instructions
  • 2. Uploaded Quick Troubleshooting and Quick Messages
  • 3. Other role-specific internal training
  • 4. Official public material only as supplemental context
  • 5. General knowledge only when the role sources do not address the point

Core rules

  • State verified facts separately from assumptions.
  • Do not generalize one county’s behavior to another county.
  • Do not collect or record customer passwords or full payment credentials.
  • When a source is flagged for current confirmation, confirm it before relying on it.

Evidence to document

  • Exact customer request
  • County and case/filing identifiers when available
  • Portal status checked
  • Reason the matter is Portal-owned or Clerk-owned
  • Referral or next action provided

Common mistakes

  • Answering a legal or procedural question because the customer phrases it as a Portal problem
  • Treating RAC status as proof of official docket placement
  • Using a county note as a statewide rule
  • Sending review-pending wording without checking current guidance

Clerk Referral / Procedural Question workflow

Verify enough Portal information to establish that the requested action belongs to the Clerk or another office.

  1. Identify the exact question and collect county, case, filing number, and case type when available. [CORE]
  2. Review Portal status only when needed to establish ownership. [CORE]
  3. Explain that Portal Support provides technical/navigational assistance and cannot provide legal advice, select forms, or change the official docket. [CORE]
  4. Do not forward emailed documents to the Clerk, Judge, or case as a filing. [CORE]
  5. Direct the customer to the Clerk in the applicable county. [CORE]
  6. Continue the Portal technical workflow before referral. [CORE] — Conditional: portalFailure = yes
  7. Record the Portal facts checked, why the issue is Clerk-controlled, and the referral provided. [CORE]

Module knowledge check

1. Which source controls when a public page and the current manager-provided workflow differ?
  1. The public page automatically replaces the workflow
  2. The manager-provided workflow remains primary unless the team explicitly changes it
  3. Whichever source is newer by webpage date
  4. General internet guidance
2. A customer asks which legal form to file. What is Portal Support’s correct role?
  1. Select the form based on the case type
  2. Provide technical/navigation assistance and refer form selection to the applicable Clerk
  3. Ask another customer what they used
  4. Change the case type in the Portal
3. How should a county-specific note be used?
  1. As a statewide rule
  2. Only after current confirmation and only for that county
  3. Only for attorneys
  4. Never documented

Module 2: Ivanti ticket handling and documentation

Foundation · 35 minutes

Handle one incident from intake through ownership, system verification, communication, resolution, or escalation.

Learning objectives

  • Claim and classify the incident correctly.
  • Record the facts and system findings needed for the issue type.
  • Distinguish customer communication from the internal Ivanti work note.
  • Close or escalate with a complete, reviewable record.

Intake and ownership

Open the complete request, assign it to yourself, identify the issue type, and collect only the identifiers required for that workflow.

  • Read the entire incident and attachments
  • Use the current queue and classification procedure
  • Search for the existing customer before creating or updating a record
  • Save after material classification or customer-record changes

Verify before communicating

A ticket note is not evidence by itself. Record what each required system showed and what troubleshooting was actually completed.

  • Name the system checked
  • Record the exact status or finding
  • Separate customer statements from analyst verification
  • Document failed and successful troubleshooting results

Closeout quality

The final record should allow another analyst to understand what happened without repeating the investigation.

  • Customer communication sent or prepared
  • Work note states systems and results
  • Outcome and current Cause Code recorded
  • Resolution or handoff summary explains why
  • Escalation includes requested Analyst action

Core rules

  • Do not mark a system complete without a finding.
  • Do not mark a troubleshooting step complete without recording the result when the result matters.
  • Use the current manager-approved Cause Code.
  • Customer-facing wording and internal notes must remain separate.

Evidence to document

  • Incident number
  • Issue type
  • Customer and required identifiers
  • Systems checked and findings
  • Communication
  • Outcome/Cause Code
  • Resolution or escalation summary

Common mistakes

  • Copying the customer’s statement into the work note as though it were verified
  • Resolving without documenting the system findings
  • Sending an internal note to the customer
  • Escalating without a clear requested action

Module knowledge check

1. What makes a system check complete in the Current Ticket?
  1. Checking the system box
  2. Recording what the system actually showed
  3. Copying the customer’s statement
  4. Adding a generic note
2. Which item belongs in the Ivanti work note rather than the customer email?
  1. A clear next step the customer can perform
  2. Internal system findings and escalation detail
  3. A polite acknowledgment
  4. A Clerk referral
3. What Cause Code should be used?
  1. The code used on the last similar ticket
  2. The current manager-approved code for the actual outcome
  3. Any code that allows resolution
  4. No Cause Code is needed

Module 3: Customer communication, Quick Messages, and Response Builder

Foundation · 30 minutes

Use reusable wording without losing accuracy, customization, or the Portal/Clerk boundary.

Learning objectives

  • Choose the correct audience: Customer Email or Ivanti Note.
  • Build a response from verified ticket facts rather than blindly copying a template.
  • Customize instructions and explanations to the actual issue.
  • Review flagged or incident-specific wording before use.

Quick Messages

Quick Messages are reusable fragments or complete messages. They save time, but they do not replace ticket review.

  • Search by intent or wording
  • Add only relevant messages to the draft
  • Remove duplicated greetings or closings
  • Edit names, identifiers, findings, and next steps
  • Do not send internal notes to customers

Response Builder

The Response Builder is for assembling and freely editing a customer response. Every block can be renamed, reordered, removed, or rewritten.

  • Start from the current ticket or blank
  • Insert verified finding and instructions
  • Explain ownership without sounding dismissive
  • Review the final free-text draft after building

A complete customer response

A strong response acknowledges the issue, states what was verified, explains responsibility, gives concrete next steps, and closes appropriately.

  • No unsupported promise
  • No legal advice
  • No internal jargon that the customer cannot act on
  • No contradictory troubleshooting steps

Core rules

  • Templates are starting points, not automatic answers.
  • Review-before-use labels are meaningful.
  • Do not include uncertain or internal-only content in a customer message.
  • Regenerate only when you intend to replace the ticket drafts.

Evidence to document

  • Verified finding
  • Customer next action
  • Portal/Clerk ownership
  • Contact status
  • Reviewed final response

Common mistakes

  • Combining several complete templates and leaving repeated greetings
  • Sending generic troubleshooting after the issue is already identified
  • Using “resolved” language before the customer has a usable next step
  • Including Analyst-only detail in a customer email

Module knowledge check

1. What is the best way to use several Quick Messages in one draft?
  1. Paste all of them unchanged
  2. Add relevant messages, remove duplicate sections, and edit the final draft to the ticket
  3. Send each as a separate email
  4. Use only the longest message
2. Which label requires extra review before sending?
  1. Quick message
  2. Review before use
  3. Customer Email
  4. Start the message
3. After manually editing a customer response, what should happen when ticket facts change?
  1. The draft should always be overwritten automatically
  2. The draft should remain intact and be marked stale until the analyst chooses to regenerate
  3. The ticket should clear
  4. The response should be sent immediately

Module 4: Registration workflow

Core workflow · 45 minutes

Process a pending registration from Ivanti intake through RAC approval or rejection and ticket closure.

Learning objectives

  • Prepare and classify the Ivanti incident.
  • Verify or complete the customer record.
  • Review the pending registration in RAC.
  • Communicate approval or rejection and close the incident correctly.

Prepare the incident

Registration work begins in Ivanti. The manager-provided workflow requires assignment, filer-role selection, field updates, an updated summary, and a verified customer record.

  • Use the current Portal Service Desk queue
  • Assign the ticket to yourself
  • Select the filer role and save
  • Update Summary to Registration – [filer name]
  • Search before creating a customer record

Review in RAC

Open RAC → Menu → Security → Registration Applications and select the correct applicant. Missing contact information may be retrieved from the pending registration and used to complete the customer record.

  • Verify name, email, phone, organizational unit, and filer role
  • Use the current manager-approved approval criteria
  • Approve with the green check or reject with the red X
  • Enter the rejection reason when rejecting
  • Save the RAC action

Communicate and close

Approved applicants are told to monitor the appropriate inbox and spam/junk folder. Rejected applicants receive the reason and required correction. The incident is then resolved with notes and the current Cause Code.

  • Do not invent approval criteria
  • Do not promise activation timing beyond the current approved wording
  • Document the decision and communication
  • Resolve only after the required action and communication are complete

Core rules

  • The exact approval criteria are not reproduced in the source set; verify current manager guidance.
  • Save after changing filer role and after material ticket updates.
  • Use the rejection reason actually recorded in RAC.
  • Do not assume Florida Bar verification is required for every registration; follow the current manager procedure.

Evidence to document

  • Filer role
  • Customer name/email/telephone
  • RAC pending application
  • Approval/rejection decision
  • Rejection reason when applicable
  • Communication and Cause Code

Common mistakes

  • Creating a duplicate Ivanti customer
  • Skipping the filer-role save
  • Approving or rejecting from memory rather than current criteria
  • Resolving before sending the approval or rejection message

Registration workflow

Process a pending Portal registration from Ivanti intake through RAC decision and ticket closure.

  1. Set the queue to Portal Service Desk All Open Tickets, open the incident, review the complete request, and assign it to yourself. [CORE]
  2. Select the filer role and save immediately. [CORE]
  3. Update the dropdowns and fields beneath Description; change Summary to Registration – [filer name]; save. [CORE]
  4. Search for an existing customer before creating or updating the Ivanti customer record. [CORE]
  5. Verify name, email, telephone, EFI – Efilers organizational unit, and filer role. [CORE]
  6. If contact information is missing, open the pending registration in RAC and update the customer record. — Conditional: addressMissing = yes
  7. Open RAC → Menu → Security → Registration Applications and select the correct applicant. [CORE]
  8. Verify the current manager-approved approval criteria before making the decision. [CORE]
  9. Approve with the green check and save. [CORE] — Conditional: decision = Approved
  10. Reject with the red X, enter the reason, and save. [CORE] — Conditional: decision = Rejected
  11. Email the filer that registration was approved and to monitor the appropriate inbox and junk/spam folder. [CORE] — Conditional: decision = Approved
  12. Email the filer the rejection reason and required correction. [CORE] — Conditional: decision = Rejected
  13. Change status to Resolved, add resolution notes, select the current Cause Code, and save. [CORE]

Module 5: Florida Bar verification and attorney-account checks

Core workflow · 25 minutes

Open the exact official profile, review only the fields needed for the ticket, and document the comparison without over-interpreting it.

Learning objectives

  • Build the exact Florida Bar profile address from a numeric Bar number.
  • Review name, Bar number, member status, eligibility, and public email when relevant.
  • Compare the public email with the ticket or Portal email.
  • Record a factual verification note and apply it to the current ticket.

Exact profile workflow

The profile address uses the numeric Bar number after the num= parameter. The Workbench opens the official page; it does not bulk-collect member records.

  • Enter the numeric Bar number
  • Open the official profile
  • Review the visible profile
  • Copy and locally parse profile text when useful
  • Confirm the imported preview against the official page

Interpret the comparison correctly

A public-email mismatch is a fact requiring review; it is not automatic proof that the Portal account is wrong or unauthorized.

  • Record both sources
  • Follow the manager procedure for the specific account type
  • Do not change the account solely because of a mismatch
  • Document what was displayed and what action was taken

Minimize data

Use only information necessary for the support incident. Do not create a separate local attorney database or collect unrelated profile details.

  • No bulk scraping
  • No unnecessary personal data
  • No automatic eligibility decision beyond the displayed profile wording
  • Human review remains required

Core rules

  • The exact profile URL is https://www.floridabar.org/directories/find-mbr/profile/?num=[BAR NUMBER].
  • The copied-text parser is a convenience, not a substitute for reviewing the official page.
  • A mismatch requires review and documentation, not automatic account action.
  • Apply the verification only after checking the preview.

Evidence to document

  • Bar number
  • Displayed name

  • Displayed eligibility
  • Public email comparison

Module 6: Account access, password, inactive, and locked accounts

Core workflow · 40 minutes

Verify the requester and RAC account before providing the current reset, activation, locked-account, duplicate-account, or escalation path.

Learning objectives

  • Verify the requester is authorized.
  • Locate the correct RAC account and record its actual status.
  • Provide the appropriate manager-approved access procedure.
  • Escalate only when Tier I cannot safely correct the account.

Verify before acting

Account support starts with requester authorization and the correct RAC account. Do not disclose or record credentials.

  • Confirm account holder or authorization
  • Record username, associated email, filer role, and status
  • Check duplicate or consolidated-account concerns
  • Use Florida Bar comparison only when the manager procedure requires it

Choose the correct access path

Password reset, pending activation, inactive message, locked account, duplicate account, and organization changes are different problems.

  • Use Forgot Password wording for a reset
  • Use the current locked-account review before reactivation
  • Do not treat every inactive message as a locked account
  • Escalate when the account cannot be safely corrected at Tier I

Document the result

The work note should state the RAC status, action taken, communication, and outcome without including a password or temporary credential.

  • No password in Ivanti
  • No credential disclosure to an unverified requester
  • Document duplicate-account findings
  • State why escalation was necessary

Core rules

  • Never ask for or record the customer’s password.
  • Do not use conflicting historical password requirements from excluded source entries.
  • Locked-account handling requires current manager guidance.
  • Account ownership and organization changes may require a different path than a routine reset.

Evidence to document

  • Requester authorization
  • RAC username/email/role/status
  • Access issue type
  • Duplicate-account finding
  • Action or escalation outcome

Common mistakes

  • Resetting the wrong account
  • Assuming an inactive message means the account is inactive
  • Sharing credentials with staff who are not the account holder
  • Using obsolete password rules

Module 7: Filing status, Workbench, My Submissions, and docket separation

Core workflow · 40 minutes

Determine whether the customer has an unfinished Workbench item, a Portal submission, a technical processing problem, or a Clerk-controlled docket question.

Learning objectives

  • Collect and use the filing number.
  • Verify the exact Portal status in RAC and My Submissions.
  • Explain why Workbench is not proof of submission.
  • Use CCIS and Clerk referral when official docket action is involved.

Identify what exists

A Workbench item, a submission, and an official docket entry are different records. The filing number and verified system status determine the path.

  • Collect the nine-digit filing number when applicable
  • Check RAC status
  • Review My Submissions
  • Use CCIS for official docket questions
  • Record Portal and docket statuses separately

Workbench limits

Manager-provided wording describes Workbench as unfinished/history-like activity. Its presence does not prove that a filing was submitted or timestamped.

  • Do not rely on a Workbench submission number as a filing number
  • Start over when the clean-retry procedure requires it
  • Do not resume a failed or stale Workbench item during a clean retry

Portal versus Clerk

The Portal transmits the filing; the Clerk controls official processing and docket placement. A transmitted or accepted Portal status must not be described as an official docket result without checking the appropriate system.

  • Explain ownership clearly
  • Refer docket correction or removal to the Clerk
  • Escalate technical processing only after evidence is complete

Core rules

  • RAC status does not prove docket placement.
  • Workbench history does not prove submission.
  • Document Portal status and official docket status separately.
  • Do not tell the customer the Clerk accepted or docketed a document unless the appropriate evidence supports it.

Evidence to document

  • Filing number
  • County/case number
  • RAC status
  • My Submissions result
  • CCIS/docket finding when applicable
  • Date/time and technical evidence for stuck processing

Common mistakes

  • Calling a Workbench item “filed”
  • Using submission number and filing number interchangeably
  • Telling the customer the Portal can change the docket
  • Escalating without a filing number or exact status

Filing Status / Docket workflow

Separate Portal submission status from Clerk-controlled docket activity and identify the correct next action.

  1. Collect the nine-digit filing number, county, case number when available, date/time, and exact concern. [CORE]
  2. Locate the filing in RAC and record the exact Portal status. [CORE]
  3. Review My Submissions or the filer-visible submission history. [CORE]
  4. Explain that Workbench history does not prove submission. [CORE] — Conditional: workbenchOnly = yes
  5. Review CCIS when the issue concerns official docket placement, case parties, or document access. [CORE] — Conditional: docketQuestion = yes
  6. Document Portal status and docket status separately. [CORE]
  7. Explain that the Portal transmits filings and the Clerk controls official docket processing. [CORE] — Conditional: docketQuestion = yes
  8. Prepare the technical escalation package. [CORE] — Conditional: technicalStuck = yes
  9. Record findings, owner, customer next action, and outcome. [CORE]

Module 8: Upload, PDF, browser, and clean-slate troubleshooting

Core workflow · 60 minutes

Collect evidence, perform the complete manager-approved clean retry, validate the document, and escalate only after documenting the results.

Learning objectives

  • Capture the exact error and environment.
  • Apply the browser/session clean-slate sequence.
  • Validate the PDF and upload behavior.
  • Create an evidence-complete escalation when the clean retry fails.

Evidence first

Before changing anything, record the exact error, date/time, filing/user identifiers, browser and version, device, document details, scope, impact, and screenshot availability.

  • Use exact error wording
  • Record whether the issue is reproducible
  • Identify whether one or multiple users are affected
  • Do not rely on “it does not work” as the escalation summary

Browser and session sequence

The manager troubleshooting sequence includes a supported computer/browser, Sign Out hyperlink, hand-keyed site address, cache/cookie clearing, browser update, pop-up/redirect allowance, clean restart of the filing, and no reuse of the failed Workbench item.

  • Do not file from a phone or tablet
  • Do not use Safari on Apple/Mac under current team guidance
  • Use the combined browser/pop-up/firewall message when applicable
  • Try another supported browser/computer and restart the machine

Document validation

Confirm PDF/PDF-A format, scanned resolution guidance, unlocked/unsecured status, no ZIP/compression, permitted filename, correct page size, and completed upload indicator.

  • Correct secured or compressed files before retrying
  • Wait for upload completion before adding the next document
  • Use specific XFA/PDF troubleshooting when applicable
  • Verify current technical limits before quoting them

Core rules

  • The clean retry must start from the beginning and not reuse the failed Workbench item.
  • Document every result, not just the steps attempted.
  • Technical limits and domain allowlists may change; follow current manager guidance.
